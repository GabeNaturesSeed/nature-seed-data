# Nature's Seed — Regenerative Ag Series Voice Guide

Derived from: How to Overseed Pasture Seed, Pasture Pig Forage Mixes, How to Plant Grass Seed, Best Grass Seed to Buy (existing high-traffic NS articles).

## 10 Voice Rules

**Rule 1 — Open with the reader's problem, not a fact.**
First sentence describes a situation the reader is already in, or a result they want. Never open with a definition or history lesson.
- Bad: "Regenerative agriculture is a set of farming practices aimed at..."
- Good: "Your hay bill keeps climbing. Your pasture has bare patches. You've heard the word 'regenerative' but assumed it was for bigger operations. It's not."

**Rule 2 — Use "you" and "your" in every paragraph.**
The reader must feel this was written for them. Use "we" only when referring to Nature's Seed as a company.

**Rule 3 — Contractions are fine.**
"It's", "you'll", "they're", "don't" — use them. Avoiding contractions makes copy feel like a government form.

**Rule 4 — Short paragraphs. Two to three sentences max.**
Digital readers scan. Break it up ruthlessly.

**Rule 5 — Every vague claim gets a number.**
"Better water retention" → "holds up to 20,000 additional gallons of water per acre [1]". Numbers build trust. Vagueness loses it.

**Rule 6 — No academic transition words.**
Cut: "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Thus," "It is worth noting that". Say the next thing directly.

**Rule 7 — Sub-headings answer a question or make a statement.**
- Bad: "Timing Considerations"
- Good: "When to Seed: The Window That Actually Matters"

**Rule 8 — Explain jargon the moment you use it.**
Never assume the reader knows what "CEC" or "rhizobia" means. Define inline with an em-dash or parenthetical.

**Rule 9 — Every CTA links to a specific product.**
"Browse cover crop seed" is lazy. "Shop crimson clover seed at naturesseed.com/pasture-seed/individual-pasture-species/crimson-clover/" is actionable.

**Rule 10 — End sections with the "so what."**
After a fact or process, tell the reader what it means for them: "That means less hay to buy this winter." "That's money you don't have to spend on fertilizer."

## 5 Voice Examples from Existing NS Articles

1. "When your pasture begins to show signs of wear, it may be time to consider overseeding." — Opens with reader's observable problem.
2. "Big-box stores often contain nearly 50% of filler material." — Specific number builds credibility instantly.
3. "All tall fescue in Nature's Seed blends is endophyte-free, eliminating risk of fescue toxicosis in horses and cattle — a condition causing weight loss and reproductive problems." — Technical term immediately explained with consequences.
4. "Animals on quality forage gain weight faster, produce more milk, and have fewer health problems than those relying heavily on stored feed." — Results framed as ROI, not abstract benefits.
5. "As a general rule, if your pasture still contains 50–70% of desirable plants, you can rejuvenate the land by overseeding." — Practical threshold, not vague guidance.
