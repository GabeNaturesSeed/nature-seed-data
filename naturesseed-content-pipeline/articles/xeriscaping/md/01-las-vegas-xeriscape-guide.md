If you're reading this with a Kentucky bluegrass front yard still drinking sprinkler water in Henderson, Summerlin, or anywhere inside the SNWA service area, the clock is no longer hypothetical. On January 1, 2027, irrigating decorative turf with potable water becomes illegal in Southern Nevada. That's the law — Assembly Bill 356, signed in 2021, with the deadline less than two years away.

The Southern Nevada Water Authority isn't bluffing. Commercial properties caught running sprinklers on banned turf after the deadline can be fined up to $5,000 per day. HOAs that drag their feet on common-area conversions are exposed to the same penalty structure. Residential homeowners face shut-off of water service in extreme cases, plus loss of the rebate they could've collected by acting early.

This is also the largest cash giveaway for landscape work in the state's history. SNWA pays $2 per square foot of turf you remove, up to 10,000 square feet per site — a maximum of $20,000. That money doesn't last forever. Once the ban takes effect, the rebate program shifts character; the easiest path is to act now, while pre-approval is straightforward and inspector backlogs are still manageable.

Here's the part most homeowners don't realize: you don't have to install rock. Seed counts.

## What the SNWA Turf Ban Actually Covers

The decorative turf ban targets *non-functional* grass. That phrase matters. SNWA defines non-functional turf as ornamental grass that exists for visual purposes only — front yards, parkways (the strip between sidewalk and curb), HOA common areas, medians, business park lawns, and the green skirts around parking lots.

What the ban does **not** cover:
- Backyard turf used for recreation (kids, pets, family activities)
- School playing fields, public parks, athletic fields, golf course playing surfaces
- Cemeteries
- Single-family side yards used as functional space

If you have a front lawn that nobody walks on, that's the target. If you have a backyard where the dog runs and the kids do soccer drills, that's allowed to stay — though SNWA still pays you to convert it if you choose to.

For HOA boards, the ban applies to all common-area decorative turf regardless of whether anyone uses it. Common-area entry monuments, perimeter strips, and ornamental medians are all on the chopping block. Boards that haven't started planning are running out of runway — most HOAs need 12 to 18 months to budget, bid, demo, and replant a community-scale conversion.

## The $2/sq ft Rebate: How Cash Actually Hits Your Bank Account

The WaterSmart program is administered by SNWA and works like this:

1. **Pre-approval.** You apply online before touching anything. SNWA confirms your turf qualifies, and you get a written approval letter. **Do not skip this step.** If you tear out turf without pre-approval, you forfeit the rebate.
2. **Demo.** Remove the existing turf — sod cut, herbicide kill, or solarize, depending on your method. Document with photos at every stage.
3. **Replant.** Install your approved drought-tolerant landscape. Seed mixes qualify, as do drip-irrigated trees and shrubs, decomposed granite, and approved hardscape. Two trees per 500 square feet is required if you go fully xeric.
4. **Establish.** SNWA gives you a window to establish the new landscape — usually 90 days from demo.
5. **Inspection.** Submit completion paperwork. An SNWA inspector visits the site, verifies the conversion meets program requirements, and approves payment.
6. **Payment.** Check arrives in 4–8 weeks.

For a 2,000 square foot front yard, that's $4,000 in your hand. For an HOA common area at 10,000 square feet, $20,000. The cap is per site, so a multi-property HOA can stack rebates across separate parcels.

## Approved Seed Species for Las Vegas

SNWA's approved plant list is long and includes hundreds of trees, shrubs, succulents, and groundcovers. What most contractors won't tell you is that grass seed is also on the list — as long as it's a low-water species adapted to the Mojave.

Here's where Nature's Seed catalog maps to what survives in Las Vegas:

| Species | Best For | Water Use vs. Bluegrass | Texture |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Common Bermudagrass](/products/common-bermudagrass/) | Active lawn replacement, high traffic | ~75% less | Medium, dense |
| [Buffalograss](/products/buffalograss/) | Low-traffic ornamental lawn, native | ~80% less | Fine, soft |
| [Blue Grama](/products/blue-grama/) | Parkways, slopes, ornamental | ~80% less | Very fine |
| [Sideoats Grama](/products/sideoats-grama/) | Edges, structural accents | ~80% less | Tall, structural |
| [Southwest Wildflower Mix](/products/southwest-wildflower-mix/) | Seasonal color, pollinators | Minimal after establishment | Variable |

**[Common Bermudagrass](/products/common-bermudagrass/)** is the workhorse for anyone replacing a lawn they actually want to walk on. It tolerates 110°F summer pavement heat, recovers from foot traffic, and uses about a quarter of the water Kentucky bluegrass demands. The trade-off: it goes dormant brown in winter from roughly mid-November through mid-March. Most Las Vegas homeowners are fine with that — it's the same dormant cycle as the surrounding desert.

**[Buffalograss](/products/buffalograss/)** is the native plains grass that built the Great Plains lawn movement. Once established (give it a full season), it asks for almost nothing — a deep watering once or twice a month in peak summer. Soft, low-growing, and silvery green when active. Goes dormant in winter the same as Bermuda. If you don't need a high-traffic surface, this is the lowest-maintenance lawn you can plant in Southern Nevada.

**[Blue Grama](/products/blue-grama/)** is the fine-textured cousin to Buffalograss. It produces those tiny eyelash-shaped seed heads that catch light beautifully in fall. Best as an ornamental groundcover in parkways and side yards rather than a play lawn.

**[Sideoats Grama](/products/sideoats-grama/)** runs taller — 18 to 24 inches — and works as a structural accent grass along fence lines, near patios, and as a transition between hardscape and wildflower beds.

**[Southwest Wildflower Mix](/products/southwest-wildflower-mix/)** isn't a lawn — it's color. Plant it in beds, along walkways, and at the edges of your grass conversion to give the property seasonal interest and pollinator value. Desert marigold, globe mallow, blanketflower, and Mexican gold poppy are typical components.

## When to Seed in Las Vegas

This is where most DIY conversions fail. Las Vegas has two narrow planting windows. Miss them and you're starting over.

**Spring window: April 15 – May 15.** Soil temperatures need to hit 60°F at a 4-inch depth before warm-season grasses (Bermuda, Buffalograss, Blue Grama, Sideoats) will germinate. April 15 is roughly when that happens in the valley. Push past May 15 and you're seeding into 100°F afternoons — germination drops, water demand spikes, and seedlings cook before they root.

**Fall window: September 1 – October 15.** This is the better window for most species, especially Buffalograss and Blue Grama. Soils are still warm from summer, days are getting shorter and cooler, and seedlings have all winter to develop deep roots before next summer's heat. Avoid seeding in July or August — monsoon downpours will wash seed off slopes and into storm drains.

For wildflower mixes, fall (October–November) is strongly preferred. The seeds need cold stratification through winter to germinate properly in spring.

## Step-by-Step: Applying for the SNWA Rebate

1. **Measure your turf.** Walk the property with a tape measure. SNWA wants square footage by area (front yard, parkway, side yard, etc.).
2. **Sketch your plan.** A simple to-scale drawing showing what comes out, what goes in, where trees will sit, irrigation layout. Doesn't need to be professional — pencil on graph paper works.
3. **Apply at snwa.com.** Submit through the WaterSmart Landscapes portal. Upload photos of existing turf, your sketch, and your contact info.
4. **Wait for pre-approval.** Usually 2–4 weeks. You'll get an approval letter with your specific terms, deadlines, and payment cap.
5. **Demo and document.** Remove turf using your chosen method. Photograph before, during, and after. SNWA inspectors will compare these to your final conversion photos.
6. **Replant.** Install your approved landscape — seed, trees, drip irrigation, hardscape — within the program's timeline.
7. **Establish.** Give seed at least 60–90 days to germinate and develop root mass before requesting inspection.
8. **Submit completion paperwork.** Final photos, receipts, plant list. Schedule the inspection.
9. **Inspector visits.** They check that what's in the ground matches what was approved. Bring patience — small notes (missing tree, wrong groundcover ratio) can delay payment.
10. **Get paid.** Check arrives 4–8 weeks after passing inspection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does seed qualify for the SNWA turf replacement rebate?**
Yes. Native and drought-tolerant seed mixes qualify as approved turf replacement under the WaterSmart program. You must receive pre-approval before demolishing existing turf, and you'll need photo documentation at each stage. Sod is not required.

**What grass grows in Las Vegas without much water?**
Buffalograss and Bermudagrass are the workhorses for Las Vegas lawns. Blue Grama and Sideoats Grama handle lower-traffic areas like parkways and slopes.

**What is the SNWA rebate amount?**
$2 per square foot, up to 10,000 sq ft per site ($20,000 maximum). The rebate is paid after a site inspection confirms the turf removal and approved replacement are complete.

**When is the SNWA turf ban deadline?**
January 1, 2027. Decorative grass irrigated with potable water becomes illegal in Southern Nevada on that date under AB356 (2021).

## A Note on HOA Rules and Decorative Turf

Nevada law (NRS 116) prohibits HOAs from blocking water-efficient landscaping in single-family residential properties — and SNWA has worked with HOAs across the valley to update CC&Rs that conflict with the ban. If your HOA is still enforcing turf-only rules in 2026, those rules are unenforceable and likely about to be illegal. Print the HOA letter from SNWA's website (it's posted publicly) and bring it to your next board meeting. Most boards have already received guidance from their management companies, but a polite reminder that the AB356 deadline applies to the HOA itself can move the conversation along.

If you sit on a board, the math is straightforward: budget conversion now while contractors and seed are available, or pay penalties later while scrambling to find both. The HOAs that have already started — many in Summerlin, Anthem, and Mountain's Edge — got first pick of contractors and the cleanest application timelines.

## Cost: Seed vs. Sod vs. Rock

A common question from homeowners running the rebate math: what does the conversion actually cost out of pocket?

| Method | Material Cost (per sq ft) | Install Labor | Net After Rebate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decomposed granite + drip + plants | $4–$7 | $3–$5 | $5–$10 (out of pocket) |
| Sod (Bermuda or Buffalograss) | $1.50–$2.50 | $2–$4 | -$0.50 to $2 (close to free or net positive) |
| Seed (Bermuda, Buffalograss, mix) | $0.10–$0.40 | $1–$2 (DIY $0) | -$1.50 to $0 (net positive in most cases) |

Seed is the cheapest path by a wide margin. For a 2,000 square foot front yard converted to seeded Bermudagrass, total material and prep cost runs roughly $400–$1,500 depending on whether you DIY the demo and prep. The $4,000 rebate clears that easily.

The trade-off is time. Seed needs 60–90 days to establish before SNWA's final inspection, and a full season before it looks like a "lawn." If you can wait, you'll come out ahead. If you can't, sod or hydroseed are faster but more expensive.

## Don't Wait Until 2027

Every homeowner who waits is fighting two clocks: the legal deadline and the contractor calendar. Demo crews and seed-and-install companies are already booking 8 to 12 weeks out in peak season. Inspectors are running long.

Pull together a plan now. Match your yard to the species in the table above. Order seed in the quantity you need (most warm-season grasses seed at 2–4 lb per 1,000 sq ft). Apply for pre-approval. The earlier you start, the less you're competing with everyone else who waited.
