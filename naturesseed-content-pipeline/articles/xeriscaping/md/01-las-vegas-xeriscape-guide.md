If you're reading this with a Kentucky bluegrass front yard still drinking sprinkler water in Henderson, Summerlin, or anywhere inside the SNWA service area, the clock is no longer hypothetical. On January 1, 2027, irrigating decorative turf with potable water becomes illegal in Southern Nevada. That's the law — Assembly Bill 356, signed in 2021, with the deadline less than two years away.

The Southern Nevada Water Authority isn't bluffing. Commercial properties caught running sprinklers on banned turf after the deadline can be fined up to $5,000 per day. HOAs that drag their feet on common-area conversions are exposed to the same penalty structure.

Here's the part most homeowners don't realize: you don't have to install rock. Seed counts — and the right seed turns into a lawn that handles 110°F summers on a fraction of the water bluegrass demands.

## What the SNWA Turf Ban Actually Covers

The decorative turf ban targets *non-functional* grass. That phrase matters. SNWA defines non-functional turf as ornamental grass that exists for visual purposes only — front yards, parkways (the strip between sidewalk and curb), HOA common areas, medians, business park lawns, and the green skirts around parking lots.

What the ban does **not** cover:
- Backyard turf used for recreation (kids, pets, family activities)
- School playing fields, public parks, athletic fields, golf course playing surfaces
- Cemeteries
- Single-family side yards used as functional space

If you have a front lawn that nobody walks on, that's the target. If you have a backyard where the dog runs and the kids do soccer drills, that's allowed to stay.

For HOA boards, the ban applies to all common-area decorative turf regardless of whether anyone uses it. Common-area entry monuments, perimeter strips, and ornamental medians are all on the chopping block. Boards that haven't started planning are running out of runway — most HOAs need 12 to 18 months to budget, bid, demo, and replant a community-scale conversion.

### Common Bermudagrass — the workhorse Vegas lawn

[Common Bermudagrass](/products/common-bermudagrass/) is the right answer for any Las Vegas yard that still needs to function as a lawn — kids, dog, foot traffic. It's heat-adapted to the point of indifference: 110°F afternoons don't faze it once it's rooted, and it recovers from wear faster than any other warm-season species we sell. Established Bermuda runs on roughly a quarter of the water Kentucky bluegrass demands and the root system pulls moisture from depths bluegrass never reaches.

The trade-off: it goes dormant tan from roughly mid-November through mid-March. Most Vegas homeowners stop noticing within a year — the surrounding desert does the same thing. Established appearance is a tight, dense, medium-fine lawn that mows clean at 1–2 inches. From a freshly seeded bed, expect 60–90 days to a usable lawn under summer temperatures.

### Buffalograss — the lowest-input lawn you can plant

[Buffalograss](/products/buffalograss/) is the native plains grass that built the original Great Plains lawn. Once established (give it a full season), it asks for almost nothing — a deep watering once or twice a month in peak summer is enough. Soft underfoot, silvery-green when active, and naturally low-growing so you can mow rarely or not at all. Goes tan-dormant in winter the same as bermuda.

If you don't need a play surface, this is the cheapest long-term lawn you can own in Southern Nevada. Allow 60–90 days for visible cover, a full season for it to fill in completely. Plant from seed at 2–3 lb per 1,000 sq ft.

## When to Seed in Las Vegas

This is where most DIY conversions fail. Las Vegas has two narrow planting windows. Miss them and you're starting over.

**Spring window: April 15 – May 15.** Soil temperatures need to hit 60°F at a 4-inch depth before warm-season grasses (Bermuda, Buffalograss, Blue Grama, Sideoats) will germinate. April 15 is roughly when that happens in the valley. Push past May 15 and you're seeding into 100°F afternoons — germination drops, water demand spikes, and seedlings cook before they root.

**Fall window: September 1 – October 15.** This is the better window for most species, especially Buffalograss and Blue Grama. Soils are still warm from summer, days are getting shorter and cooler, and seedlings have all winter to develop deep roots before next summer's heat. Avoid seeding in July or August — monsoon downpours will wash seed off slopes and into storm drains.

For wildflower mixes, fall (October–November) is strongly preferred. The seeds need cold stratification through winter to germinate properly in spring.

## A Note on the SNWA Rebate

SNWA's WaterSmart program pays $2 per square foot of removed turf, up to 10,000 sq ft per site ($20,000 max), and seed conversions qualify alongside rock-and-shrub conversions. If you want the rebate, you have to apply *before* removing any turf — demoing first forfeits the program entirely. Check current rates and approved species at snwa.com.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What grass grows in Las Vegas without much water?**
Bermudagrass and Buffalograss are the workhorses for Vegas lawns. Blue Grama and Sideoats Grama handle lower-traffic areas like parkways and slopes. All four are native or naturalized to the desert Southwest and use 75–80% less water than Kentucky bluegrass once established.

**Can I seed instead of laying sod?**
Yes. Seed is the cheapest path by a wide margin — material costs run roughly 10–20% of sod for the same area. The trade-off is establishment time: 60–90 days to a usable lawn from seed versus immediate from sod. For most homeowners, the savings are worth the wait.

**Does SNWA accept seed for the rebate?**
Yes. Both seed and sod conversions qualify if the species are on the approved list and you receive pre-approval before removing existing turf.

**When is the SNWA turf ban deadline?**
January 1, 2027. Decorative grass irrigated with potable water becomes illegal in Southern Nevada on that date under AB356 (2021).

## A Note on HOA Rules and Decorative Turf

Nevada law (NRS 116) prohibits HOAs from blocking water-efficient landscaping in single-family residential properties — and SNWA has worked with HOAs across the valley to update CC&Rs that conflict with the ban. If your HOA is still enforcing turf-only rules in 2026, those rules are unenforceable and likely about to be illegal. Print the HOA letter from SNWA's website (it's posted publicly) and bring it to your next board meeting.

If you sit on a board, the math is straightforward: budget conversion now while contractors and seed are available, or pay penalties later while scrambling to find both. The HOAs that have already started — many in Summerlin, Anthem, and Mountain's Edge — got first pick of contractors and the cleanest application timelines.

Seed is the cheapest path by a wide margin. For a 2,000 square foot front yard converted to seeded Bermudagrass, total material and prep cost runs roughly $400–$1,500 depending on whether you DIY the demo and prep.

The trade-off is time. Seed needs 60–90 days to establish, and a full season before it looks like a "lawn." If you can wait, you'll come out way ahead. If you can't, sod is faster but more expensive.
