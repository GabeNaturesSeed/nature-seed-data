If you live in the Phoenix metro and you've been told there's "an AMWUA rebate" for ripping out your front lawn, the truth is more annoying than that. There is no single AMWUA rebate. AMWUA is a coalition of ten member cities — Phoenix, Mesa, Scottsdale, Tempe, Gilbert, Chandler, Glendale, Peoria, Surprise, and Avondale — and each runs its own program. Tucson Water is separate from AMWUA entirely.

But the species that thrive across the low desert are the same regardless of which utility writes you a check: Common Bermudagrass for active lawns, Sonoran Desert Wildflower Mix for color, Sideoats Grama for structural accent, plus Southwest Wildflower Mix as a perennial-heavy companion. All four are on every AMWUA city's approved-plant list and on Tucson Water's.

This guide leads with the seed. The city-by-city paperwork is at the bottom.

## Low-Desert Arizona Seed Lineup

### Common Bermudagrass — the Phoenix-metro lawn

[Common Bermudagrass](/products/common-bermudagrass/) is the standard lawn-replacement turf for the Phoenix metro and Tucson, approved by every AMWUA program as a water-conserving turf alternative compared to higher-water cool-season grasses. It handles full Phoenix summer heat with minimal water once established — established appearance is a tight, dense, medium-fine lawn that mows clean at 1–2 inches.

Goes dormant tan from late November through mid-March. Some homeowners overseed with annual ryegrass for winter color; most learn to embrace the dormant tan — it matches the surrounding desert. Establishes from seed in 3–6 weeks under summer temperatures.

### Sonoran Desert Wildflower Mix — purpose-built for the Phoenix metro

[Sonoran Desert Wildflower Mix](/products/sonoran-desert-wildflower-mix/) is purpose-built for the Phoenix metro climate and the Sonoran Desert ecotone. Annual and perennial natives bring seasonal color through the cooler months — Mexican gold poppy, desert marigold, blanketflower, owl's clover, lupine — and support pollinators (Sonoran Desert bees, hummingbirds, butterflies).

Why it works here: every species in the mix is regionally adapted. Sow in fall (September–October) for late-winter and spring bloom; this is the standard low-desert wildflower window. Critical for HOA front-yard applications because it provides the "color" boards often want to see in approved plans. Helps hit ground-cover thresholds in parts of a converted area not filled by Bermudagrass or structural natives.

### Southwest Wildflower Mix — perennial-heavy companion

[Southwest Wildflower Mix](/products/southwest-wildflower-mix/) is the perennial-heavy companion. Where the Sonoran Desert mix carries more annuals for fast color, the Southwest mix provides longer-term groundcover from established perennials. A good base mix for large converted areas where you want plantings that come back year over year without reseeding.

Order Southwest Wildflower Mix at [Nature's Seed](/products/southwest-wildflower-mix/).

### Sideoats Grama — structural bunchgrass accent

[Sideoats Grama](/products/sideoats-grama/) is the Texas state grass but is also native to Arizona — it ranges from Texas through the Southwest into northern Mexico — and qualifies as an approved native on most AMWUA program lists. It's a structural bunchgrass: clumping, with sideways-hanging seed heads. Used as a feature planting in mixed-native designs rather than as a mowed turf substitute. Drought-adapted, slow to establish, durable once mature.

A common low-desert layout: Bermudagrass for the active lawn area, Sonoran Desert Wildflower Mix for slopes and parkways, decomposed granite around foundation plantings, Sideoats Grama as accent grass, drought-tolerant shrubs (creosote, brittlebush, palo verde, mesquite) for vertical structure.

## Seeding Windows for AMWUA Cities (Phoenix Metro)

- **Spring (March 15 – April 30):** Primary window for warm-season grasses, especially Bermudagrass. Soil is warm enough for germination, daytime temperatures are mild enough not to fry seedlings.
- **Fall (September 1 – October 15):** Primary window for native grasses and wildflower mixes. The Sonoran Desert Wildflower Mix is fall-sown specifically.
- **Avoid summer seeding (May 15 – August 31):** Phoenix summer heat shuts down germination for most species and bakes seedlings before they develop a root system.

## Arizona's HOA Xeriscape Protection: ARS § 33-1816 and § 33-441

Two state statutes protect homeowner rights to install drought-tolerant landscaping. ARS § 33-1816 (planned communities) and § 33-441 (broader real estate) prohibit HOAs from blocking xeriscape installations. HOAs can require reasonable design standards (clean edges, defined planting areas, maintenance plan) — they cannot say no outright. Arizona courts have consistently sided with homeowners on xeriscape disputes when the plan meets reasonable design standards.

Per-square-foot rates vary by city — Scottsdale runs the most generous program at ~$1.50/sq ft, most others sit between $0.20 and $0.50/sq ft. Check the specific city's website for current rates; AMWUA city programs update amounts regularly.

A note on service-address vs. mailing-address: in the East Valley especially, mailing addresses can sit in one city while water service comes from another. Look up the utility name on your water bill, not the city on your driver's license.

## A Note on Tucson Water (Separate from AMWUA)

Tucson Water is the City of Tucson's utility and runs its own conservation programs independent of AMWUA. The relevant programs for landscape conversion: Water Harvesting Incentive Program (rainwater harvesting and graywater systems), Rainwater Harvesting (passive earthworks), and Turf Reduction (rebates for converting irrigated turf to xeriscape). Visit tucsonaz.gov/water for current program details.

The seed lineup above applies to Tucson — [Common Bermudagrass](/products/common-bermudagrass/), [Sideoats Grama](/products/sideoats-grama/), [Sonoran Desert Wildflower Mix](/products/sonoran-desert-wildflower-mix/) — though many Tucson conversions go full xeric without a turf substitute given the climate.

## Three Patterns That Trip Up AMWUA Applicants

1. **Pre-approval is required — do NOT remove turf first.** Every AMWUA member city, and Tucson Water, requires you to submit the application and receive pre-approval before doing demo work. Demo performed before pre-approval forfeits the rebate. There is no retroactive path. If your contractor says "we'll just rip it out and apply later," fire your contractor.

2. **60-day establishment window before final inspection.** Most AMWUA programs require at least 60 days between completion and final inspection. Don't schedule too early; an inspection that fails because cover is incomplete sends you back to the start of the documentation cycle.

3. **Bring or upload seed bag labels at completion.** Inspectors verify species at inspection time. With seed, the inspector needs documentation — the seed bag tag with the species name, the receipt from your seed supplier, or a photo of the bag uploaded to the application portal. Save your seed bags. Photograph the labels.

The most common rejection reason at final inspection: plant cover below the program's threshold. Cities typically require 60–70% live ground cover. If your seeding hasn't filled in (germination was patchy, irrigation was uneven), the inspector will reject. Avoid this by giving seeded conversions a full 60–90 days of establishment, watering consistently through the first growing season, and overseeding any thin patches before the inspection date.

## Putting It Together

Pick the right city program for your service address, get pre-approval before you touch the turf, plant climate-appropriate species in the right window, document the establishment, and pass inspection at the 60-day mark. The rebate clears in 4–8 weeks after passing.
