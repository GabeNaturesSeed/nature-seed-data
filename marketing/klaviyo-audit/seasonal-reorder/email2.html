How to prepare your lawn for spring seeding

Three things our seed specialists recommend before you seed:

1. Soil temp check
Cool-season grasses (fescue, bluegrass, rye) germinate best at 50–65°F soil temp. Warm-season (bermuda, zoysia) need 65–70°F+.
2. Scalp and dethatch
Mow low, rake out thatch. Seed-to-soil contact is the #1 germination factor. Dead thatch blocks it.
3. Starter fertilizer
High-phosphorus starter fert in the seedbed significantly improves germination rates — especially in clay soils.

Spring pasture overseed: what actually works

Three things our agronomists see make the biggest difference:

1. Rest the field first
Remove livestock for at least 2 weeks before overseeding. Hoof traffic on newly germinating seed is a silent killer.
2. Use a slit seeder
Broadcast seeding on established sod rarely works. Slit seeders place seed below the thatch layer where moisture is consistent.
3. Seeding rate matters
Most people under-seed. For overseeding: use 80% of new-planting rate. For bare patches: full rate.

How to get wildflowers to actually germinate

Most wildflower failures come down to three fixable mistakes:

1. Weed competition
Bare soil invites weeds. Solarize or till before seeding to deplete the weed seed bank. Wildflowers can't out-compete weeds at germination.
2. Planting depth
Most wildflower seed needs light to germinate. Press seed into soil surface — don't bury it. A hand roller after seeding is ideal.
3. Consistent moisture in week 1
Light daily watering for 7–10 days after seeding dramatically improves establishment. Don't let the surface dry completely.

3 ways to get better germination this spring

Our seed scientists recommend the same three steps across almost every seed type:

1. Seed-to-soil contact
The most overlooked factor. Press or rake seed into the surface — don't scatter on top of thatch or mulch.
2. Consistent early moisture
Light daily watering for the first 7–14 days. Let germination start before reducing frequency.
3. Right rate for the job
Overseeding into existing growth requires 80% of a new-planting rate. Bare ground needs the full rate.
